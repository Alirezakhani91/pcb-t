INSIGHTS / TAX PREPARATION

A practical Canadian small-business tax-time checklist

Get your records, reconciliations and supporting documents in order before tax deadlines become urgent.

A WAVE ABOVE THE REST
THOUGHTFUL FINANCE • CLEARER HORIZONS

A smoother tax season usually starts long before a return is due. Good preparation means knowing where your records are, whether accounts have been reconciled and which questions still need to be answered.

1. Confirm the period and the records you need

Check the relevant fiscal period, filing responsibilities and deadlines for your business structure. Organize sales records, supplier invoices, bank statements, credit-card statements, payroll summaries and any year-end adjustments relevant to your situation.

2. Reconcile the accounts before preparing figures

Make sure bank, card and payment processor balances have been reviewed. Follow up on unreconciled differences, missing receipts and unusual transactions. A clean reconciliation makes later questions easier to answer.

3. Keep GST/HST support accessible

Where GST/HST applies, organize sales and purchase records, information supporting input tax credits and copies of filed returns. Supplier invoices may need particular details to substantiate eligible claims. The correct treatment depends on the transaction and applicable rules.

4. Review payroll and major transactions

Bring together payroll records, relevant deduction information and year-end slips if the business has employees. Identify equipment purchases, loans, owner contributions, unusual payments or other transactions that may need a specific accounting or tax review.

5. Prepare questions before the handoff

List unresolved items and provide the supporting documents alongside a clear explanation. Do not assume that every payment is deductible or that every GST/HST amount is recoverable; those conclusions depend on the facts and current rules.

Your preparation checklist

  • Current general ledger and reconciled accounts.
  • Sales, purchase and expense support with readable dates and details.
  • GST/HST and payroll records where relevant.
  • Outstanding questions, adjustments and significant transactions flagged.

This is general educational information. Requirements differ for corporations, sole proprietors, charities and other organizations. Confirm your circumstances with an appropriately qualified Canadian tax professional.
