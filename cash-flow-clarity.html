Cash flow and profit: what owners need to know

Profitability and available cash are different measures. Understanding both helps business owners plan payments, spending and growth.

A business can report a profit and still struggle to pay its bills on time. That does not necessarily mean the books are wrong—it may mean cash receipts and cash payments are happening at different times.

Profit measures performance. Cash flow measures timing.

Profit compares revenue earned with expenses recognized over a period, using the accounting method applied to the business. Cash flow tracks when money actually enters or leaves the bank. A sale invoiced today may improve reported results before the customer pays.

Why a growing business can feel short of cash

Consider a contractor who pays suppliers and employees before receiving a progress payment, or a retailer buying inventory ahead of a seasonal rush. Revenue may be healthy while cash is tied up in receivables or stock. Loan repayments and major equipment purchases can also affect cash differently from profit.

Watch working capital, not only sales

Start with three practical questions: How quickly do customers pay? Which bills will come due before collections arrive? How much cash is committed to inventory or recurring costs? These questions often explain pressure that a profit-and-loss statement cannot show on its own.

Build a simple rolling cash forecast

List opening cash, expected customer receipts, payroll, supplier payments, rent, debt payments and relevant tax obligations. Review the forecast weekly or monthly, depending on how quickly your cash position changes. Distinguish confirmed payments from assumptions and adjust the forecast as new information arrives.

What to review each month

  • Profit-and-loss results alongside actual cash movement.
  • Accounts receivable and overdue customer balances.
  • Upcoming payroll, supplier and GST/HST payments as applicable.
  • Large planned purchases and seasonal changes.

A forecast is a planning tool, not a guarantee. This article provides general information and should not replace advice tailored to your business.
