Cash flow vs. profit: why both deserve your attention

Two valuable views of business performance, and why they tell different stories.

Start a conversation ↗
A WAVE ABOVE THE REST
THOUGHTFUL FINANCE • CLEARER HORIZONS

A business can be profitable on paper and still have cash pressure. Understanding the difference makes planning more useful.

Profit and cash answer different questions

Profit concerns business performance over a period. Cash flow is about the timing of money entering and leaving. The same transaction may affect those views at different times.

Timing creates pressure

Sales made on credit may not provide immediate cash for expenses. Likewise, advance payments or large purchases can change cash balances without telling the full profitability story.

Make the forecast a habit

Even a simple forecast can list expected cash coming in and key payments going out. Review assumptions regularly; forecasts are planning tools, not guarantees.

Practical next steps

  • Profit shows income minus expenses over a period.
  • Cash flow shows when money actually moves in and out.
  • Late customer payments can affect cash even during strong sales periods.
  • A simple forward-looking cash forecast helps surface possible gaps.
  • Use both views together instead of relying on either in isolation.
